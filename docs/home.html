How a bet gets on the card

Most games never make it. That's on purpose.

1

Price every game

Each sport has its own model. Team strength, injuries, rest, weather and the players who swing a game (goalies, starting pitchers) become an expected score.

2

Compare with the sportsbooks

Prices come from DraftKings and FanDuel with the sportsbook's built-in margin removed, so the model and the market are compared like for like.

3

Bet only the clear gaps

A bet needs a big enough gap, then it's sized at 1, 2 or 3 units. Daily caps keep the card short, so most games get no bet.

4

Grade everything

Picks lock before the game. Final scores grade them the next morning, and nothing is edited afterward. Wins and losses both stay on the page.

Is any of this guaranteed to win?

No. Sportsbooks are sharp and even a good model has losing weeks. The site shows how each model is really doing so you can size your bets to match.

What is a unit (u)?

A unit is one standard bet. A common rule of thumb is 1 to 2 percent of your betting bankroll. A 2u play risks twice as much as a 1u play.

Why does the NHL say tracking only?

We replayed the hockey model on a full past season. It roughly broke even and its best betting rule failed on games it hadn't seen, so it earns no bets until this season's results say otherwise. It still shows its leans and grades them.

Where do prices come from, and how often does this update?

DraftKings and FanDuel, through The Odds API. The site rebuilds every morning at 11 AM Eastern, hockey locks its card about an hour before the first puck, and baseball checks closing lines through the day. Lines move, so check the price in your app before you bet.

Is this betting or financial advice?

No. It's model output shared for information and fun. Only bet what you can afford to lose.
